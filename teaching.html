Teaching

Courses, Salons, and Consultations
with James F. Mulhern

One page for the whole of the teaching work — the literary salons, the ten-session creative-writing course, the memoir consulting, and the self-paced resources for adult writers and secondary-school teachers.

How the work is offered: All courses, salons, and consultations are taught in person in Philadelphia or the Philadelphia region. There is no Zoom, no hybrid, and no remote delivery. The self-paced resources listed further down are separate — a small library of digital guides available at any time.

Section ILive Teaching — In Person, Philadelphia

Courses meet at libraries, bookstores, community centers, universities, retirement communities, and literary nonprofits in the Philadelphia region, and on request elsewhere in the Northeast. Every one of them meets in a single room.

The 2601 Salon — Literary Course Catalog

A rotating catalog · single sessions to sixteen-session courses

A working catalog of literary courses for adult readers and writers — close reading, generous conversation, and a light writing prompt at the close of most sessions. Full sixteen-session courses, four-session workshops, single evening talks, and one-day masterclasses are all available. Titles include Women in American Literature, The Greatest Short Stories Ever Told, Minority Voices, and Literature & American History, among others.

Format
Weekly ninety-minute sessions; adapted to fit a partner venue's calendar.
Audience
Adult readers and writers; also delivered as programs for libraries, bookstores, retirement communities, and literary nonprofits.
Location
In person, in the Philadelphia region.

The Art of Telling

Ten-session creative-writing salon · short story, poetry, memoir

A ten-session salon that moves in three movements — the short story, the poem, and the memoir. Each session includes a brief historical framing, a passage read aloud, a guided discussion, and a ten-minute in-class writing exercise done by hand in a paper notebook. No grades, no quizzes, no required publication outcomes — only the work, the company, and the slow accumulation of skill.

Format
Ten Sunday afternoons, ninety minutes each.
Audience
Adult writers, community-room residents, and readers ready to draft in three genres.
Location
In person, in Philadelphia.

Memoir Consulting

One-to-one work with writers of memoir and personal essay

Private consulting for writers working on a memoir or a book-length personal essay project. The work covers market fit, structure and shape, the ethics of writing about living people, and clear editorial next steps. A first orientation of twenty to thirty minutes is offered without charge; manuscript review, custom outlines, and written follow-up are paid engagements.

Format
Free 20–30-minute orientation; paid manuscript review, outline work, and written editorial follow-up.
Audience
Memoirists and personal-essayists at any stage — first draft to book proposal.
Location
In person, in Philadelphia. No Zoom or phone sessions.

Pathways to Publication

A standalone lecture for aspiring writers

A single seventy-five-minute lecture, approximately thirty slides, on the real spectrum from traditional to legitimate hybrid to self-publishing — with a plain accounting of who pays whom, and how to recognize a vanity operation dressed in "hybrid" costume. Suitable as a bookstore evening, a library program, or a one-off talk for a writers' group.

Format
Seventy-five minutes, one evening.
Audience
Aspiring writers weighing how and where to publish.
Location
In person, in the Philadelphia region.

Section IIWho Comes Through the Room

The teaching work is shaped for four kinds of reader in particular. Most sessions hold two or three of them at once.

Adult readers and writers

Readers who want a serious literary community and writers of any stage who want a room to read carefully and draft in.

Memoirists and personal-essayists

Writers with a book-length personal story to tell who want structural, ethical, and market-fit guidance from a published novelist and editor.

Older adults and community-room residents

The Art of Telling and the salon catalog have been shaped again and again with retirement-community residents in mind, and with them in the room.

High-school and college students

Teens preparing for AP English, college essays, and literary analysis; occasional cross-generational rooms alongside the adult courses.

Program partners and institutions

Libraries, bookstores, universities, retirement communities, and literary nonprofits bringing a course to their community.

Secondary-school ELA teachers

Teachers looking for classroom-ready close readings, discussion questions, and craft-focused literary guides — served through the self-paced resources below.
